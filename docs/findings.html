What ~2,000 auction seasons say about winning your draft

Almost nothing about how much you spend, where you spend it, or when you spend it predicts winning. What predicts winning is what your roster looks like in December. Spend only matters insofar as it converts into roster — and the conversion rate, not the spend, is the skill.

+0.4 Grade A

Draft-class value-over-replacement is the top controllable draft signal, across every format tested. In auction superflex it runs up to +0.7.

2 good QBs

In superflex, finishing with two good QBs predicts winning (+0.23 to +0.37). What you spend on QB predicts nothing (≈0, confirmed null three times).

Two more durable edges

  • Lineup efficiency is a trait, not luck. Setting your best lineup predicts residual wins (+0.45) and persists year to year (+0.48) — in every format tested.
  • Conversion beats concentration. Managers who turn dollars into starters win at any budget shape; piling money into one tier without converting adds nothing.

What players to target

  • Buy elite production in the middle of the auction. The median eventual top-10 scorer cost $19 — only 2.7% of top-10 finishers cost $41+. A
  • RB $21–40 is the sweet tier: 80.7% starter hit rate with the best value per dollar. WR value concentrates $6–20. QB at $41+ is near-certain (96.6% hit) but you're paying full price for certainty — mid-tier QBs return more per dollar. A
  • Throw $1–2 darts. End-of-draft $1–2 picks hit starter tier ~22% of the time. Cheap depth has real yield. A
  • Buy production on good offenses. Picks at $10+ on top-third offenses hit starter tier 88.7% vs 58.0% on bottom-third offenses. A
  • Don't pay for expensive youth. Players 24 and under at $25+ busted 22.8% vs 14.4% for prime-age players. A
  • Injury history is a real flag. Players who missed 5+ games the prior season hit at 63% vs 74% for durable players at $10+. The market does not fully price it. A
  • Avoid expensive end-of-auction leftovers. Among $20+ picks, the last quartile of nominations returns the least value per dollar. Nominate your expensive targets early. B

Where the money goes

  • Champions spend like everyone else. Title teams' budget shares are field-shaped — roughly 31% RB / 36% WR / 21% QB / 13% TE — with a tilt toward a cheap QB1 (~12% of budget). Nobody outside one league wins by stacking 47% on RB; that's a local fingerprint, not a format law. A
  • Track conversion, not allocation. Share of spend landing on eventual starters predicts points at +0.42. Positional spend shares, top-heavy concentration, and early-vs-late timing don't differentiate winners. B+
  • Keep catastrophic overpays small. Dollars burned on ≥$25 picks that finish below replacement is a real, scored behavior (−0.25). In auction redraft, under ~$15/season is the controllable target. A
  • In-season: hold FAAB for late. Late-season deployment helps (+0.18). Total FAAB spent is mildly positive but it's an activity marker, not a lever — "spend more" is not the advice. And churning the wire is fine everywhere it was tested. B

Killed by the data

  • "Spend big on QB in superflex." Spend is null; finishing with two good QBs is the thing. The most expensive mistake in the draft room is conflating the two.
  • "Target young players." At $25+, youth busts more, not less.
  • "Schedule luck decides seasons." Points scored predict wins at 0.82 vs schedule luck at 0.52, in every league tested. (One league where luck dominated is the outlier, not the rule.)
  • Strength of schedule as a draft input. Tested, null, validated.
  • The "$40 QB is title poison" rule. Didn't replicate outside its home league — but there's no edge there either, so don't pay for the story.
  • Exact budget formulas (47% RB, $15 bust line) as universal laws. League-specific framing; the cross-league versions are the counts and behaviors above.

How this was built. Hypotheses were frozen in writing before touching fresh data, then confirmed cold on new leagues — twice. Corpus: 37 public Sleeper chains plus 108 targeted 10-team auction superflex league-seasons, ~2,000 auction manager-seasons and ~41,000 auction picks total. Grades: A = confirmed across cold waves with information fraction ≥1; B = confirmed but thinner or noisier; killed = falsified or null-validated. A few patterns (churn penalty, luck-over-points, exact bust-dollar lines) hold only in the home league's history and are labeled as such — they don't travel.

Figures are correlations and hit rates from completed seasons, 2018–2026. They describe what won, not a guarantee.
